Pre-event call, written 7 Nov 2026

Is the market pricing Rémy Cointreau's China and tariff risk correctly?

My short answer is no. The share price already assumes a recovery close to my bull case, while the evidence on China points the other way. On 25 November Rémy reports half-year results and new medium-term targets. I wrote down what I expect before that date, and I will score it after.

Fundamental view

Lean short. Probability-weighted value about €37, roughly 28% below the share price.

Into 25 November

Buy volatility, not direction. I expect a move of about 4.7%; the options price about 4.1%.

Thesis

1. The China cognac rebound looks more like shipments than demand. Cognac grew 10.3% organically in the first half, driven by deliveries to China before the Mid-Autumn Festival, and the share price fell anyway. Having lived in Shanghai until October 2026, my read is that premium gifting in China goes to baijiu such as Moutai, not cognac, and that the anti-corruption code keeps official banquets and expensive alcohol in check. The second half also faces a hard comparison: cognac grew 15.5% in January to March 2026.

2. Part of the margin recovery is mechanical, and the market already prices more than that. Gross margin fell from 70.6% to 65.8% last year. About 2.7 points came from tariffs and 0.9 points from inventory write-downs. With tariffs falling from about €25m to about €5m, roughly 2 points should come back on their own. The stock trades at 16x last year's EBITDA, close to my bull case, not my base case (about 13x).

3. The turnaround plan's €100m is public, and "more volume" would not add value. RC Forward targets about €100m of extra operating profit by 2028-29. If Rémy chases volume with price adjustments, my model gives about 10% more sales by 2030-31 but roughly the same operating profit, and almost the same value per share. What matters on 25 November is the profit target, not the sales target.

4. The balance sheet leaves little room. Net debt was 3.3x EBITDA in March 2026, Moody's rates the company Baa3 with a negative outlook, and Rémy issued a €250m hybrid bond at 6.75% in September.

Valuation

A five-year DCF built from the 2025-26 annual report, with a WACC of 7.8% (cost of equity 8.9%, about 20% debt). Each scenario has its own assumptions for cognac and liqueur growth, gross margin, marketing spend, overheads and the cash tied up in ageing cognac stock. The terminal value is 76 to 81% of enterprise value, so the result is sensitive to the long-run margin: in the base case, plus or minus 0.5 points of WACC moves the value by roughly €4 to €5 per share.

Peers tell the same story. On last reported figures, Pernod Ricard trades at 9.9x EBITDA, Diageo at 11.5x and Campari at 12.7x. Rémy trades at 16.1x, a 39% premium to the peer median, and at 30x earnings against 15x. At the peer median EV/EBITDA, Rémy would be worth about €33 a share. Some premium is deserved: Rémy is a pure luxury cognac house with years of ageing stock, and its earnings are close to a cyclical low, which makes trailing multiples look high. But the size of the premium is exactly what the bull case has to justify.

What the options market prices

Rémy options trade on Eurex. The 20 November expiry ends before the results and the 18 December expiry includes them, so the extra variance in December is the market's price for the event. From delayed quotes on 7 November, that is an expected move of about 4.1% on 25 November (between 2% and 5% given wide spreads).

History says it depends on the type of day. Across 22 results days since 2022, a November half-year results day moved the stock about 2%, because sales are already public in October. Days with strategy news moved it far more: +5.9% in June 2025, +7.2% when RC Forward launched in April 2026, +9.2% in June 2026.

My expected move is a little above what the market charges, so the trade I would put on is a long December straddle rather than a directional bet. The edge is small and the quotes were delayed, so I will recheck it with live prices before 23 November.

Catalysts

25 November 2026: half-year results and RC Forward medium-term targets. The two things to watch are the gross margin (does it rise with lower tariffs?) and whether the new targets are about profit or only about sales.

Expected late January, April and June 2027: third-quarter sales, full-year sales and full-year results, based on Rémy's usual calendar.

Risks to the call

China depletions turn out to be real. If sell-out to consumers, not just shipments, keeps growing, the bull case gets much more likely.

Cocktails in China. Cocktail bars are spreading in Chinese cities and Cointreau is a cocktail staple. Rémy does not disclose Cointreau sales by country, so this upside is invisible in the numbers today.

US destocking ends quickly. Rémy says US depletions were roughly flat in the first half. If the inventory cut is over, liqueur sales could rebound faster than I assume.

Family control. Rémy is controlled by the Hériard Dubreuil family. Any corporate move on the shares would hurt a short position regardless of fundamentals.

What I got wrong

7 Nov 2026, before the event. My first read was that the options were underpricing 25 November, because recent Rémy events moved the stock 6 to 9%. The full history showed that November results days usually move it only about 2%. Whether the options are cheap depends on how much the new targets surprise, not on the past average.

The results of the call will be added after 25 November.
