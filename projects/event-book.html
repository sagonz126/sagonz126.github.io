How good are my calls before earnings?

Before a company reports, I write down probabilities for what will happen and compare them with what the options market prices. After the release, I score every call, right or wrong. The point is the process: if I am wrong, the reasoning should still have been sound.

How it works

Before the release I publish four things: the move the options price for the day, how the stock moved on past results days, the evidence for and against, and a probability for each question. Every page is committed to a public GitHub repository, so the time stamp shows it was written beforehand.

After the release each probability gets a Brier score: the squared gap between my probability and what happened (1 if it happened, 0 if not). Zero is perfect, and always saying 50% scores 0.25. Below 0.25 means the calls added something.

Open call, written 11 Nov 2026

Richemont, half-year results, 13 November 2026

Richemont owns Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels. Jewellery is about three quarters of sales. The results come out at 07:00 CET on Friday 13 November. The shares traded at about CHF 175 on 11 November (SIX Swiss Exchange, ticker CFR).

What the options price

A move of about 6.3% on the day.

What history says

6.9% on average on November results days. Fairly priced, so no volatility trade.

What the market prices

Richemont has a weekly option that expires on Friday 13 November, the results day itself, so its price is almost all about the event. The at-the-money straddle cost about CHF 11 on 11 November, or 6.3% of the share price (between 5.0% and 7.6% given the bid-ask spread, on delayed quotes).

Unlike some companies, Richemont's half-year results are real news: second-quarter sales and the margins come out at the same time. A 6 to 7% move is normal, and the options price about that.

The evidence

For a strong quarter. Richemont beat sales expectations at each of its last four updates; in the first quarter sales grew 20% at constant exchange rates against about 11% expected. Hong Kong is where it shows most: retail sales of jewellery and watches rose 19.7% in July, 11.9% in August and 20.3% in September. From my own time in Hong Kong, the queues outside Chow Tai Fook and the European jewellers were constant. Chinese buyers want both: local gold brands for tradition, and European maisons such as Van Cleef and Cartier to show wealth and taste. Gold averaged $4,262 an ounce in July to September, 23% higher than a year earlier, which also lifts the price of each piece.

Against. The comparison is harder: sales grew 14% in the second quarter last year, against 6% in the first. Peers slowed: LVMH's watches and jewellery grew 6% in July to September, and Hermès said Greater China dipped. Swiss watch exports to China fell 14% in September. On margins, higher gold prices raise the cost of every piece, and US tariffs on Swiss goods now apply at 15% for the whole half-year. In the second half of last year the operating margin was about 18%, against 22.2% in the first half.

Wild card. Richemont is reported to be in talks to join the consortium bidding for De Beers. Investors may not like a jeweller putting money into diamond mining.

What would prove me wrong

Second-quarter growth below 10%, a drop in Hong Kong and Macau after the strong summer data, or a gross margin that holds up despite gold, which would mean price increases did more than I assume.
