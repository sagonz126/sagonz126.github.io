How good are my calls before earnings?

Before a company reports, I write down probabilities for what will happen and compare them with what the options market prices. After the release, I score every call, right or wrong. The point is the process: if I am wrong, the reasoning should still have been sound.

How it works

Before the release I publish four things: the move the options price for the day, how the stock moved on past results days, the evidence for and against, and a probability for each question. Every page is committed to a public GitHub repository, so the time stamp shows it was written beforehand.

Reading the options. A straddle is a call and a put at the same strike, so it pays off if the stock moves far in either direction. Its price, as a share of the stock price, is roughly the move the market expects. When the first option expiry after the release also covers some normal days, I take out the variance of those normal days to isolate the results day. Then I compare that number with how the stock actually moved on past results days. If history moved more than the options price, the options look cheap, but only if the gap is wider than the bid-ask spread.

After the release each probability gets a Brier score: the squared gap between my probability and what happened (1 if it happened, 0 if not). Zero is perfect, and always saying 50% scores 0.25. Below 0.25 means the calls added something.

Open call, written 11 Nov 2026

Burberry, half-year results, 12 November 2026

Burberry is two years into a turnaround under its CEO, Joshua Schulman. The results come out at 07:00 London time on Thursday 12 November and cover the six months to late September. The key new number is comparable store sales for July to September, the second quarter of its financial year. The shares traded at about 1,094p on 11 November (London Stock Exchange, ticker BRBY).

What the options price

A move of about 4.6% on the day.

What history says

6.9% on average, 5.3% median. Cheap at the mid price, but the spread eats the edge.

What the options price

Burberry options trade on ICE Futures Europe. The first expiry after the release is Friday 20 November, so the option covers the results day plus six normal trading days. Delayed quotes for the 1,100p strike on 11 November:

How I get from 6.0% to 4.6%. The straddle prices the whole period to 20 November, not just Thursday. Six normal days at Burberry's recent daily volatility (1.9%, from the last 30 days) account for part of the price. Taking their variance out of the total leaves an expected move of about 4.6% for the results day itself.

How much I trust it. Not much. The quotes are delayed, the spread is wide, and open interest at this strike is close to zero, so the range runs from 3.1% to 5.9% depending on where you trade. Implied volatility for the stock is about 34% a year against about 31% realised over the last 30 days, so the options charge only a small premium for the event.

12 of the 19 days moved more than the 4.6% priced. But 2024 and early 2025 were extreme: a profit warning, a CEO change and the new plan. The last four days averaged 5.2%, closer to the price.

Would I buy the straddle? At the mid price, it breaks even if the stock ends more than about 6% away from 1,100p by 20 November. Results days alone did that 8 times out of 19. At the ask, it needs about 7%, which happened 6 times out of 19. The options look cheap against the full history, but not by enough to pay the spread on quotes I cannot trade live. So no trade: the view goes into the last probability below instead.

What I saw in China. Burberry is very visible in Shanghai: stores across the city, and people like to be seen wearing it. Fakes are getting more common, which tells me the brand is wanted. Younger buyers go for the puffers and belts. Wealthier men and women buy the clothing, scarves, shoes and bags. Burberry itself credits Gen Z for its China growth, which matches what I saw.

Two limits on that view. Fakes show demand for the brand, not sales for Burberry; some of that demand never reaches a Burberry till. And most puffer sales come after September, so they matter more for the January update than for this quarter. What my view does support is that China growth is real demand, not a one-quarter bounce. That is why I put the China question at 50% even though the base is 7 points harder.

Open call, written 11 Nov 2026

Richemont, half-year results, 13 November 2026

Richemont owns Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels. Jewellery is about three quarters of sales. The results come out at 07:00 CET on Friday 13 November. The shares traded at about CHF 175 on 11 November (SIX Swiss Exchange, ticker CFR).

What the options price

A move of about 6.3% on the day.

What history says

6.9% on average on November results days. Fairly priced, so no volatility trade.

What the market prices

Richemont has a weekly option that expires on Friday 13 November, the results day itself, so its price is almost all about the event. The at-the-money straddle cost about CHF 11 on 11 November, or 6.3% of the share price (between 5.0% and 7.6% given the bid-ask spread, on delayed quotes).

What history says

November half-year results (+11.1%, −5.0%, −6.4%, +4.9%)6.9%
Results days since 20256.0%
A normal day1.4%

Unlike some companies, Richemont's half-year results are real news: second-quarter sales and the margins come out at the same time. A 6 to 7% move is normal, and the options price about that.

The evidence

For a strong quarter. Richemont beat sales expectations at each of its last four updates; in the first quarter sales grew 20% at constant exchange rates against about 11% expected. Hong Kong is where it shows most: retail sales of jewellery and watches rose 19.7% in July, 11.9% in August and 20.3% in September. From my own time in Hong Kong, the queues outside Chow Tai Fook and the European jewellers were constant. Chinese buyers want both: local gold brands for tradition, and European maisons such as Van Cleef and Cartier to show wealth and taste. Gold averaged $4,262 an ounce in July to September, 23% higher than a year earlier, which also lifts the price of each piece.

Against. The comparison is harder: sales grew 14% in the second quarter last year, against 6% in the first. Peers slowed: LVMH's watches and jewellery grew 6% in July to September, and Hermès said Greater China dipped. Swiss watch exports to China fell 14% in September. On margins, higher gold prices raise the cost of every piece, and US tariffs on Swiss goods now apply at 15% for the whole half-year. In the second half of last year the operating margin was about 18%, against 22.2% in the first half.

Wild card. Richemont is reported to be in talks to join the consortium bidding for De Beers. Investors may not like a jeweller putting money into diamond mining.
