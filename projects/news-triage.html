Can a free AI tell which headlines move a stock?

Four times a day, a free AI model reads the news about 40 large European companies and says which headlines will move the shares, in which direction, and by how much. Every evening the calls are checked against what the market actually did. Nothing is tested on old news, so the model cannot already know the ending.

How it works

  1. 1CollectEvery six hours, new English headlines for each company from Google News and Yahoo Finance.
  2. 2LabelGemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, on Google's free tier, reads each headline and answers: is it relevant, which way, how big, will volatility rise.
  3. 3CommitLabels are saved to a public GitHub repository before the market closes, so the time stamp proves they came first.
  4. 4ScoreAfter the close: the stock's move against the European market, in units of its own normal daily move.

A move counts as notable above one normal day and big above two. "Normal" is each stock's own: 2% is big for Nestlé and noise for Rolls-Royce. The prompt is frozen as version 1; any change starts a new, separately scored experiment.

    Limits, and what went wrong

    It reads news, it does not trade it. A story often reaches Google News after the market has started to react, so this measures how well the AI reads headlines, not a strategy anyone could run.

    Matching is noisy. "Shell", "SAP" and "ABB" are common words, and searches return lifestyle pieces and option-chart pages. The AI's first job is to throw those out; on day one it rejected 41% of what came in.

    Market-adjusted, not risk-adjusted. Moves are measured against the STOXX Europe 600 with a beta of one.

    Day one, 11 November 2026. The first news source, GDELT, refused almost every request from GitHub's servers ("too many requests"), so I switched to Google News and Yahoo Finance. The first model timed out on batches of 60 headlines; the pipeline now uses the faster Flash-Lite in batches of 25. Yahoo no longer serves one of Roche's share lines, so Roche uses the other. All of it is in the commit history.
